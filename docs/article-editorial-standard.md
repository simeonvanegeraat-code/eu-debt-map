# EU Debt Map editorial and writing standard

Established: 2026-10-10. Operational review: 2026-10-21. Applies to new articles
and substantive rewrites in all locales. Use alongside [the publication workflow](article-discover-workflow.md)
and [the image standard](article-image-style.md).

## Editorial promise

Help readers understand European public finances and prices, question a plausible
assumption, and investigate the evidence themselves. Write with pace, clarity,
and a recognizable point of view where appropriate. Accuracy takes precedence
over a striking headline, a preferred opinion, or search traffic.

Our voice is accessible financial journalism: concrete stakes, direct openings,
active verbs, and well-explained numbers. Requests for a newspaper-like style
mean these general qualities, not imitation of an individual writer, invented
reporting, or exaggerated crisis language.

## 1. Decide what kind of article this is

- **News:** what happened, when, and what is confirmed? Separate forecasts from outcomes.
- **Analysis/explainer:** how does it work, and what explains the numbers?
- **Opinion:** what should readers believe or reconsider, and why? Make the
  position recognizable and distinguish interpretation from established fact.

Do not disguise an opinion as neutral reporting. Do not manufacture a contentious
position for a straightforward explainer. The genre should guide the opening,
structure, evidence, and public labeling under the site's conventions.

### The genre changes the editing task

| Genre | What must arrive early | What must earn its space |
| --- | --- | --- |
| News | The verified development, its source, reference period and significance. Distinguish when it was published from when it happened or was measured. | Background, calculations and comparisons that explain this development. A separate thesis, counterargument, FAQ and concluding recap are not compulsory. |
| Analysis/explainer | The reader's question and the mechanism or answer that resolves it. | Examples and detail that clarify the mechanism, including plausible alternative explanations where relevant. |
| Opinion | A recognizable, evidence-backed judgment and the stakes for the reader. | The reasoning and strongest reasonable objection; do not manufacture an opposing side or merely announce that an objection exists. |

These are editing priorities, not fixed article templates. `news-analysis`
requires analysis as well as an identifiable news development; do not label an
explainer as `news` merely to avoid a review warning. Stop when the reader's
question is answered. A complete short news report can be better than a padded
long one; no genre has a compulsory word count or number of subheadings.

## 2. Complete an editorial brief before drafting

Keep a short working brief. It is a decision aid, not boilerplate to publish.

```text
Locale and geographic scope:
Genre:
Intended reader and their main question:
Current hook, event date, and why this matters now:
New article or update? Existing related URLs and distinct reader intent:
Primary destination page and the useful action readers can take there:
Two or three possible angles, with reasons for choosing one:
Thesis in one sentence (or main answer for news/explainers):
Original contribution: calculation, comparison, explanation, or reported finding:
Two or three essential supporting claims:
Strongest reasonable objection and our response (opinion); competing explanation or missing context if relevant for other genres:
What evidence would weaken the judgment or change the main answer?
Uncertainties, missing evidence, and boundaries:
Reader takeaway:
Likely structure and approximate length:
Responsible editor/reviewer and actual review status:
```

Choose the thesis after checking the evidence. If a requested angle is contradicted
by the sources, explain the problem and propose a defensible alternative.
An interesting hook cannot rescue an unsupported premise.

Use a new URL when the article answers a meaningfully different question. Update
an existing URL when it serves the same reader intent and needs substantive
revision. Do not silently replace a requested new article with an old-article
update. Explain the recommendation if a different approach is warranted.

A dated news report may remain a truthful record of its original period. A new
statistical release can warrant a new report with a distinct development, while
the old story receives a dated follow-up link if useful. A page promising the
current position must not retain an obsolete present-tense conclusion. Correct
errors transparently; do not silently turn an archived Q1 report into Q2 news,
reset its original publication date, or rewrite the archive just to look fresh.

## 3. Build a source and claim ledger

Before writing around a consequential number or assertion, record:

```text
Claim:
Value, unit, geographic scope, and reference period:
Status: observed / provisional / estimate / forecast / original calculation:
Primary source URL and specific table, series, or passage:
Source publication date and actual access/review date:
Calculation inputs and reproducible method, if applicable:
Caveats and whether the source directly supports the wording:
Verification status and who or what checked it:
```

Prefer Eurostat, national statistics offices, central banks, and official budget
documents. Use secondary reporting for context and clearly attributed reporting.
AI output, search snippets, and an earlier EU Debt Map article are not independent
proof. Open and inspect the underlying source. Never invent quotations, interviews,
anecdotes, studies, or URLs. Do not claim a source was checked when it was not.

Check current claims against current sources. A forecast's future reference year
is not its publication date. Missing data must remain missing unless a documented,
explicitly labeled model permits estimation. Do not refresh generated data files
as a routine part of writing.

When a statistical release revises earlier periods, compare endpoints from the
same source vintage and recalculate differences, ranks and model rates. Separate
revisions from a new quarter's movement. Do not relabel unchanged annual data or
forecasts as newly observed quarterly results. Put any qualification that could
change the headline's meaning beside the claim, not only in the sources footer.

### Economic distinctions that must survive editing

- Inflation is the rate of price change; lower inflation does not reverse earlier
  price increases. Cumulative changes compound rather than simply add.
- National consumer-price measures and the harmonised European measure can differ.
  Identify the measure, use consistent comparisons, and explain unfamiliar terms.
- Averages do not establish an individual household's experience. Claims about
  public fear, attention, or expectations need evidence, not just a price chart.
- Government debt is a stock; a deficit, annual borrowing, and interest expenses
  are different flows. Debt-to-GDP is not debt per person.
- Distinguish percent from percentage points, nominal from real amounts, and the
  EU from the euro area. The ECB's 2% medium-term euro-area target is not a monthly
  national ceiling.

## 4. Test the argument before polishing it

For opinion, identify the strongest reasonable counterargument, not a convenient
straw man. State what it gets right and where the evidence supports our different
conclusion. Explain mechanisms; correlation alone does not establish causation.

For example, a chart showing that inflation has fallen cannot prove that people
are merely paying more attention since the pandemic. Persistent price increases,
household spending patterns, and measured expectations are competing explanations.
A defensible article can explore that tension without dismissing readers' experience.

Ask whether the piece offers something beyond a rewritten press release or the
destination page. One useful comparison or carefully bounded argument is better
than several weak claims. Remove claims the dossier cannot support.

## 5. Draft for a reader, not a template

Open with the real tension, a verified concrete detail, or the main finding.
For opinion, make the thesis clear early, usually within the first 150 words.
Do not begin every piece with a date, an acronym, or a generic announcement that
the subject is important.

Use short paragraphs, varied sentence lengths, and descriptive subheadings when
they help navigation. Give each paragraph a job. Put a number next to its meaning
and avoid stacking unrelated statistics. Roughly 650–1,100 words can suit a focused
opinion, but there is no minimum word count for SEO or mandatory section sequence.

Explain technical language at first use, or avoid it. For a general Dutch reader,
“volgens de Europees geharmoniseerde inflatiemeting” can be clearer than opening
with “HICP”. If the abbreviation is needed, expand and explain it once. Do not
write “volgens Europa” when that obscures which institution and measure are meant.

Illustrative wording, not a claim about current figures:

> Minder inflatie betekent niet dat de kassabon weer goedkoper wordt. Als prijzen
> eerst met 10 procent stijgen en daarna met 2 procent, kost een mandje van
> 100 euro uiteindelijk 112,20 euro.

Do not repeat a fixed formula of introduction, table, FAQ, and conclusion across
every article. Avoid stock transitions, inflated stakes, gratuitous rhetorical
questions, and repeated “not X, but Y” constructions. A dash is punctuation, not
proof of AI authorship; edit for rhythm rather than banning individual characters.

Read the title, opening, headings and ending alongside the three recent related
articles inspected for the brief. Repeated facts and necessary definitions may
stay; recurring rhetorical hooks should be reconsidered. Do not replace one
formula with a quota for different formulas. Do not assume every financial story
needs a paradox, crisis frame or a hypothetical household example.

Keep the commissioning scaffolding out of the prose. Usually state the objection
itself rather than writing “the strongest counterargument is”; replace transitions
that only announce precision or importance with the actual evidence, or cut them.
Retain direct source attribution and necessary uncertainty. Explain an important
limitation where it first matters and repeat it only when omission would mislead;
do not dilute it or turn every paragraph into a defensive methodological aside.

End with a consequence, a qualified judgment, or a practical next question.
Do not merely repeat the introduction or finish with a promotional slogan.

## 5a. Edit for specificity, voice, and forward movement

Good writing is not synonymous with human authorship. Our goal is clear,
distinctive, trustworthy journalism, not a disguise for how a draft was produced.
Apply the following craft principles in a separate prose edit. They are editorial
tools, not a rigid recipe or proof of authorship. Their research background and
limitations are recorded in [the craft research note](article-writing-craft-research-2026-10-10.md).

### Eight qualities to develop

1. **A specific question.** Name the tension that makes this article worth reading.
   “Inflation matters” is a topic; “Why does falling inflation not feel like relief?”
   is a reader question. State an evidence-backed answer rather than collecting
   everything related to the subject.
2. **Meaningful concrete detail.** Replace abstract language with a verified price,
   budget decision, comparison, or clearly labeled hypothetical example. A detail
   earns its place by explaining something, not by adding cinematic decoration.
3. **Visible reasoning.** Show the step between a number and the judgment drawn
   from it. Name an assumption or limit where it matters. Readers should be able
   to disagree with the interpretation without losing track of the facts.
4. **Forward movement.** Each paragraph answers a question, adds evidence,
   explains a mechanism, or challenges a conclusion. Summarize its job in the
   margin; merge or cut paragraphs that merely repeat an earlier job. Transitions
   should connect ideas, not just announce another section.
5. **Purposeful rhythm.** Vary sentence length with the thought. Give a difficult
   explanation room; let a short sentence land a consequence. Prefer a clear
   subject and a precise verb. Do not turn the entire piece into clipped slogans,
   and do not impose sentence-length quotas across languages.
6. **A restrained, empathetic voice.** Explain as to an intelligent non-specialist.
   Recognize why a reader's concern might be reasonable before qualifying it.
   Our voice is curious about evidence and willing to reach a bounded judgment;
   it is neither a bureaucratic report nor a manufactured angry columnist.
7. **Selective evidence.** Choose the figures that carry the argument and explain
   their significance. Keep supporting detail available through sources or the
   tool. Never remove essential caveats to improve pace or force dramatic certainty.
8. **An earned ending.** Finish with what follows from the analysis: a judgment,
   unresolved choice, or useful next question. The final paragraph should leave
   the reader further along, not recycle the opening or merely ask for a click.

### Before and after: illustrative Dutch prose

These are writing demonstrations, not reported observations or current data.

**Abstract:** “De ontwikkeling van het prijsniveau heeft aanzienlijke gevolgen
voor de financiële situatie van huishoudens.”

**More direct:** “Als boodschappen duurder worden en je inkomen niet meestijgt,
kun je met hetzelfde geld minder kopen.”

The second version identifies the mechanism and its condition rather than
announcing unspecified consequences.

**Unsupported psychology:** “Mensen denken ten onrechte dat de inflatie nog hoog
is, omdat ze sinds corona bang zijn geworden.”

**A defensible distinction:** “De inflatie kan dalen terwijl het leven duur blijft.
Wie naar de kassabon kijkt, beantwoordt een andere vraag dan wie de jaarlijkse
prijsstijging vergelijkt.”

The revision avoids diagnosing people's emotions without evidence. Any claim
about actual current inflation still needs a source and reference period.

**Empty transition:** “Daarnaast is het belangrijk om naar de lonen te kijken.”

**A logical transition:** “Maar prijzen vertellen maar de helft van het verhaal.
Of je minder kunt kopen, hangt ook af van wat er met je inkomen gebeurt.”

This connects the next section to the reader's question rather than introducing
a checklist item. It is an example, not a sentence to reuse in every article.

### Required prose-edit record

Before marking the writing ready, record a short editorial note:

```text
The main question and answer, each in one sentence:
The strongest concrete detail, and why it matters:
The reasoning step most likely to confuse a reader, and how it was clarified:
The weakest or repetitive passage, and what was cut or rewritten:
The strongest objection, and where it receives a fair response (opinion); relevant alternative explanation or context for other genres:
Opening/structure compared with three recent related articles (or all if fewer):
Read-aloud pass: awkward phrasing, rhythm, and local idiom corrected:
Ending: what it adds beyond the introduction:
Human reader feedback: actual feedback, or explicitly not performed:
Remaining limitations:
```

Use exact before/after wording or a named deletion for actual revisions, rather
than a generic claim that the text was polished. If no revision is needed for a
check, say why; do not invent changes to complete the record. For a news report,
also check that the opening identifies what is newly known without confusing the
publication date with the measurement period. For opinion, check that the ending
follows from the evidence rather than simply repeating the preferred position.

A read-aloud pass means checking how the language sounds when spoken; a model's
simulated pass must be described as such, not as human listening. Where an actual
reader is available, ask: “What is the main point?”, “Where did you have to reread?”,
and “Which claim was unconvincing?” Use the answers to revise. Reader feedback is
useful evidence of comprehension, not proof of general audience satisfaction.

Do not insert typos, fake personal memories, invented shopping trips, unverified
quotes, or forced colloquialisms to look human. Do not use an AI-detector score as
a writing-quality gate. First-person reporting requires real attributable work;
an opinion or transparent calculation does not require a fabricated eyewitness.

## 6. Make titles and internal links earn their place

The title must accurately express the finding or argument. A striking contrast is
useful only when the evidence supports it. No invented urgency, withheld essential
information, or stronger certainty than the body can defend.

Link to the relevant inflation or country page at the point where readers benefit
from inspecting the data. Describe what they can compare and, when relevant, the
period or selector to use. Use localized, verified URLs. Do not promise a chart,
calculation, or filter that the destination does not provide.

Do not force keywords or a fixed number of links. This standard supplements rather
than overrides the technical publication checks and metadata requirements.

### Link placement: reader flow and transparent sourcing

Project-owner preference, recorded 2026-10-11; applies to all article locales.

- Prefer useful internal links in the running text, placed where the destination
  helps the reader understand or investigate the subject. Do not replace a source
  link with an unrelated internal link merely to keep a visitor on the site.
- Put external source links in a clearly labeled sources section at the end of
  the article. A native expandable section is acceptable if its label is visible
  and keyboard-accessible. Ensure the links actually render: the JSON `sources`
  field alone is not a visible source list in every article template.
- Keep meaningful attribution such as “according to CBS” or “the ECB warns” in
  the prose, and retain source URLs, review dates and caveats in the source ledger
  and structured article fields. Moving links does not reduce research standards.
- Make an inline external-link exception only when direct access is essential
  to understanding the claim or taking the action discussed, such as examining
  a disputed document or accessing an official application. Record the reason
  in the editorial brief; do not impose an arbitrary link quota.
- Apply this during new writing and substantive revisions. Do not retroactively
  rewrite the entire archive without a separate request.

## 7. Adapt country editions; do not just swap country names

Dutch, French, and German editions need locally relevant evidence, comparisons,
terminology, and reader stakes. English may take a European perspective; it is not
automatically a UK edition. Check local decimal conventions and natural phrasing.

Preserve the meaning of claims and qualifications during translation. A new local
argument needs new verification. Do not assume substantially different country
articles are equivalent translations for hreflang purposes; follow the existing
translation mapping and protected-URL rules.

## 8. Use AI with distinct editorial passes

1. **Research and brief:** collect sources, identify possible angles, and flag gaps.
2. **Draft:** write from the verified dossier, not from model recollection.
3. **Skeptical edit:** challenge the thesis, counterargument, originality, and title.
4. **Verification:** recheck consequential claims against sources and reproduce
   calculations. Rereading the same AI answer is not independent verification.
5. **Line edit:** apply section 5a, improve rhythm, precision, transitions, and
   local language, and record the actual prose fixes and any reader feedback.
6. **Publication review:** record what was actually checked, unresolved issues,
   and the real responsible reviewer. Follow the publication authorization rules.

AI may produce full drafts here; this is our chosen workflow, not a claim that
all newspapers permit it. Material generation must not be described as only
spelling correction. Apply honest disclosure under the site's conventions and
flag any gap before publication rather than inventing an attribution.

Never claim that a named human read, verified, or approved the content unless that
actually happened. Authorization to deploy is not evidence of a human reading the
article. If human review remains outstanding, state that explicitly. Do not change
site-wide authorship or disclosure settings as an incidental writing task.

### Learning from other publications

When style research is part of the task, record the reference articles and the
transferable techniques observed: focus, attribution, structure, explanation,
rhythm and endings. Learn general craft across multiple examples rather than
copying distinctive wording, metaphors or the voice of one named writer. Public
AI policies do not establish the authorship of an individual article. Do not call
a reference “best read” without audience evidence, or infer that its prose caused
its reach. Test an adopted technique against our readers' question, not against
how closely it resembles a newspaper. Evidence and limitations of this review
are recorded in [the 21 October operational review](editorial-standard-review-2026-10-21.md).

## 9. Quality gates and presentation review

An unsupported consequential claim, misleading attribution, fabricated evidence,
or unchecked calculation blocks publication regardless of prose quality.

Mechanical checks and editorial review are separate gates. The article audit can
check fields, dates, source-link syntax and image dimensions; it cannot establish
that a linked source supports a claim, that the prose is persuasive, or that a
human reviewed it. Its non-news warning below 600 words is only a prompt to review
depth, never a length requirement. Short `news` reports do not receive that
generic warning. Neither silence nor a green build is editorial sign-off.

### Compact editorial sign-off

Use the existing brief, claim ledger and prose-edit record as evidence; do not
create another duplicate dossier. Record each decision as ready, revise or
unverified, with a specific reason:

1. **Promise:** the title and opening deliver the chosen genre and reader question.
2. **Evidence:** consequential claims and calculations match inspected sources,
   periods and coverage; limits that affect the meaning are visible.
3. **Progression:** every retained paragraph adds a fact, reason, explanation or
   necessary qualification; remove scaffolding and redundant recaps.
4. **Voice:** concrete language, useful rhythm and an earned ending; compare recent
   related pieces for repeated hooks without imposing a template.
5. **Reader path:** localized links have a genuine purpose, and narrow-screen
   presentation remains usable.
6. **Accountability:** record actual AI contributions, source checks and human/
   native-language review status. Never upgrade “unverified” into “passed”.

Unsupported consequential facts block publication. Missing independent human
feedback remains an explicit limitation, not a fabricated approval; publication
authorization still follows the project rules. A repeated AI pass is not an
independent reader test. Do not invent a numeric quality score to close a gate.

Assess the following dimensions with a short reason and a concrete improvement:
accuracy and sourcing; original contribution; argument and fairness; readability;
local relevance; useful internal links; and mobile presentation. An “8/10” is an
editorial judgment, not a measured guarantee. Do not award every dimension eight
simply because that was requested, and do not imply a score predicts traffic.

For visible article changes, inspect desktop and narrow mobile layouts. Avoid wide
comparison tables where short cards, prose, or a chart communicate better. If a
table is essential, verify its actual behavior on mobile, including captions and
long labels. Verify chart units, periods, accessible explanation, and source.
Follow the image standard; generated illustrations must not masquerade as real
documentary photography.

Run the checks required by the publication workflow and report actual results.
Say whether the outcome is a new article or an update, which locales are complete,
and whether it is local, deployed, or independently verified live. Never use
“published” merely because a local draft or build exists.

## Reusable assignment

```text
Prepare an EU Debt Map article using docs/article-editorial-standard.md and the
publication workflow. First inspect related articles and the destination page.
Build a concise editorial brief and a verified claim ledger. Compare possible
angles and select a defensible thesis, including its strongest objection.
Then draft in the target locale with a direct opening and original data insight.
Perform a genre-appropriate skeptical edit, source verification, and a language
edit as separate passes. Complete the section 5a prose-edit record and compact
editorial sign-off; compare recent related articles for repetitive writing.
Do not invent reporting or review. Include useful localized internal links and
inspect mobile presentation. Report remaining limitations,
the actual review status, and the checks performed. Publish only when authorized.
```
