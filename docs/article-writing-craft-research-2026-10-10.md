# Readable journalism: craft research and editorial application

Reviewed: 2026-10-10. Question: which qualities of good writing can EU Debt Map
deliberately develop, rather than merely asking for prose that sounds human?

## Evidence and its limits

Writing quality and authorship are separate questions. The sources below describe
craft or reader behavior; they do not establish a reliable way to identify human
authorship. A human byline does not itself establish quality, and fluent generated
prose does not establish reporting or understanding.

### Focus, specificity, and rhythm: practitioner guidance

[Roy Peter Clark, Poynter, *If I Were a Carpenter*, 14 August 2002](https://www.poynter.org/archive/2002/if-i-were-a-carpenter/)
recommends concrete details, strong verbs, and varying sentence length to control
pace. [His Poynter interview on short writing, 2025](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2025/roy-peter-clark-prize-excellence-short-writing/)
emphasizes focus and careful revision. These are experienced practitioners'
recommendations, not controlled evidence that a particular sentence pattern
increases engagement. Poynter's indexed source text was available during this
review; direct page retrieval returned an error, limiting inspection of the pages.

### Voice: a relationship with the reader

[Madeline Bodin, Nieman Storyboard, *The power and purpose of a writer's voice*,
30 March 2021](https://niemanstoryboard.org/2021/03/30/the-power-and-purpose-of-a-writers-voice/)
reports Mark Kramer's advice on narrative voice: understandable, empathetic, and
restrained rather than insistently formal or showy. This concerns narrative
journalism, so transferring it to data-led financial opinion is our editorial
adaptation. It does not justify inventing scenes or first-person experience.

### Online reading: empirical usability evidence

[Morkes and Nielsen, *Concise, SCANNABLE, and Objective*, 1997](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/concise-scannable-and-objective-how-to-write-for-the-web/)
report three studies involving 81 users in total. The measurement study compared
five versions of a site with 51 participants; concise, scannable, non-promotional
writing improved measured usability in that setting. It did not compare AI and
human authors, establish an ideal article length, or test our opinion articles.
Non-promotional wording is not the same as having no editorial opinion.

[Kate Moran, *How People Read Online: New and Old Findings*, 5 April 2020](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-people-read-online/)
summarizes eye-tracking research spanning 13 years and more than 500 participants.
Scanning remained common, while motivation and task affected reading depth.
Descriptive headings, early information, and plain language help readers navigate.
The report also describes cases where inline interruptions disrupted sustained
reading. This is not evidence that every reader scans or that every long article
or inline element is harmful.

## Our editorial application, not a proven formula

For EU Debt Map, apply eight qualities: a distinct question; meaningful concrete
detail; visible reasoning; progression between paragraphs; varied rhythm; a
restrained, empathetic voice; selective evidence; and an ending that advances
the argument. These synthesize the sources with our factual and editorial needs.
They are not an experimentally validated eight-factor scale.

Use a separate prose edit and record specific fixes, rather than declaring a
draft “human” or assigning automatic scores. Review the three most recent related
articles to catch repeated openings and structures without banning useful terms.
If an actual reader is available, ask them to explain the main point and identify
confusing passages. Do not claim this reader test happened unless it did.

Do not add deliberate errors, fake testimony, arbitrary sentence variation, or
detector-evasion tricks. Genuine original reporting requires real observation,
interviews, or document work; a language edit cannot supply those experiences.
