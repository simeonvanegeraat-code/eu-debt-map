# Newsroom AI and opinion writing: research note

Reviewed: 2026-10-10. Scope: public newsroom policies, reported applications,
research on AI use, and lessons for EU Debt Map. This is not an audit of private
newsroom workflows.

## What the evidence supports

Public policies and reported practice are different kinds of evidence. Neither
reveals the prompt, drafting process, or authorship of an individual column.

| Source | Supported finding | Important limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Mediahuis 2024 annual report](https://annual-report.mediahuis.com/2024/2024/naar-een-centraal-technologieplatform) | De Telegraaf and De Standaard tested NewsSearch: archive research, summaries, headline suggestions, background, timelines, and possible angles. | This historical self-report does not establish that opinion columns are mostly AI-written. A planned rollout is not proof of subsequent implementation. |
| [Mediahuis AI guidelines](https://contributors.mediahuis.ie/assets/Mediahuis%20AI%20guidelines.pdf) | AI should support journalists; generated material needs human checks, accountability, and appropriate disclosure. | Group guidance is not evidence of how every individual article was produced. The PDF's publication date was not established in this review. |
| [NRC editorial code](https://nrccode.nrc.nl/hoe-we-werken) | AI may help process information, suggest headlines, and improve sentences. NRC explicitly prohibits having AI write its articles or generate news images. | This is NRC's policy, not a universal newspaper rule. |
| [AP policy update, 23 July 2026](https://www.ap.org/the-definitive-source/announcements/ap-updates-newsroom-standards-for-artificial-intelligence/) | Approved uses include early research, summaries, transcription, translation, headline suggestions, and language assistance. Journalists review and edit output; material AI involvement calls for transparency. | AP is a news agency, not a model for opinion commissioning. Its current policy should not be confused with older guidance. |

### What research says about opinion pieces

[Russell et al., *AI use in American newspapers is widespread, uneven, and
rarely disclosed*, version 4, 26 April 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18774v4)
examined roughly 186,000 articles from about 1,500 US newspapers, using an AI
text detector. The authors estimate that about 9% were partly or fully generated.
In a separate set of roughly 45,000 opinion pieces from three major papers,
opinion was 6.4 times as likely to be flagged as news from those papers.

These are detector-based estimates, not verified authorship records. They cannot
establish the exact contribution of AI, reliably identify the process behind a
particular text, or support a claim about De Telegraaf. The study concerns US
publications and a historical sample, not all journalism in 2026.

**Conclusion:** there is evidence of newsroom AI assistance and research suggesting
substantial use in opinion writing. This review found no substantiation for the
claim that De Telegraaf's opinion articles are almost entirely AI-written.

## Writing lessons, distinct from AI policy

[NRC's seven opinion-writing tips, published in 2013](https://www.nrc.nl/nieuws/2013/10/26/zeven-tips-voor-het-schrijven-van-opiniestukken-1310289-a710639)
emphasize a direct opening, concrete examples, a recognizable position, and a
focused subject. These are enduring editorial principles, not evidence of a
modern AI workflow.

Our editorial inference is that speed and fluent wording are not sufficient.
For EU Debt Map, the useful differentiator is an argument grounded in inspectable
official data, with a clear explanation of what those data do and do not mean.
This is an editorial choice, not an experimentally proven traffic strategy.

## Recommended approach for EU Debt Map

Allow AI research assistance and full draft generation, while clearly recognizing
that this differs from NRC's policy. Separate drafting from source verification,
argument testing, and final editorial responsibility. Do not describe an
AI-generated draft as mere spelling assistance. Never invent a human reviewer or
imply that permission to publish means the owner has read the text.

Use an accessible, energetic financial-journalism voice without copying a named
columnist or treating sensationalism as a quality signal. Build each opinion
around a defensible thesis, its strongest objection, and a useful reader takeaway.
Link to the inflation or country tool where readers can investigate the argument,
not as a substitute for having an argument.

The operational standard is in [article-editorial-standard.md](article-editorial-standard.md).
These recommendations do not change published content or constitute a promise
of Google Search rankings or Discover distribution.
